## Legal basis to check before adoption

POPIA section 14 requires purpose-based retention, with lawful exceptions, access to decision records for a reasonable period where no prescribed period applies, and secure deletion or de-identification when retention is no longer authorised. Restrict use of records retained only as evidence. See [POPIA, sections 13–14](https://www.justice.gov.za/legislation/acts/2013-004.pdf).

Employment Services Act section 16 requires private employment agencies to maintain a register of registered work seekers, placements and relevant employers, and retain the covered records for at least three years. Confirm which HiredFlex activities and fields fall within that register, applicable commencement/amendments and any other employment, contractual, tax or dispute obligations before setting deletion dates. See [Employment Services Act, sections 16–17](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Acts/Public%20Employment%20Services/Employment%20Services%20Act%202014.pdf). The three-year minimum does not justify retaining every field for three years. This draft does not establish HiredFlex's registration or legal classification.

## Minimisation, restricted information and team access

Proposed intake rule: request contact details, target role and relevant source-backed employment/qualification facts only. Do not request full ID/passport numbers, bank details, home street address, photographs, race, health/disability details or other unnecessary attributes for routine CV preparation. ID numbers and ordinary photographs are personal information, not automatically the statutory category of special personal information. Race, health and biometric information engage POPIA sections 26–33; document the applicable authorisation before any necessary exception. Do not infer these attributes from names or images. [POPIA primary text](https://www.justice.gov.za/legislation/acts/2013-004.pdf), sections 10, 26–33.

If unsolicited material contains excess information, restrict access, ask for a redacted replacement and remove unnecessary working copies through a reviewed plan. Preserve originals only where a documented lawful need/hold requires it; never alter evidence to invent or conceal qualifications. Record the source and redaction action without repeating the removed data. No automatic redactor or erasure process is implemented.

Proposed access: Neo/accountable operator controls grants; assigned intake staff see minimum contact/consent metadata; assigned drafter and independent QA reviewer see necessary source/draft material; employers/recruiters receive only authorised role-relevant information, not CRM-wide access. Keep current database company/assignment checks intact. Use individual accounts, MFA where supported, restricted attachment sharing and no public candidate links. Before real intake, record named operators and permissions. Remove/revoke access and linked devices promptly on departure or role change, review shared links/exports, rotate shared secrets if any, and record completion. No team roster, existing access audit or offboarding automation is claimed.

## Information Officer and security compromises

Known legal requirement: POPIA section 55(2) requires registration before an Information Officer takes up the section's duties; the Information Regulator confirms registration for private/public bodies. Neo must confirm the actual responsible party and appropriate officer/delegation, complete registration and securely retain the acknowledgement. No registration number, appointment or filing is asserted here. [Regulator guidance and registration entry](https://inforegulator.org.za/).

Proposed response owner: Neo as operational incident coordinator until the responsible-party/registered-officer arrangements are confirmed; do not call him a registered officer without evidence. Staff escalate suspected compromise immediately. Contain exposure, restrict affected access, preserve minimal logs/evidence and record discovery, affected systems/data, actions and notification decisions. The responsible party remains accountable; its Information Officer/deputy coordinates human notification to the Regulator through the eServices portal and to affected people as required. Where there are reasonable grounds to believe personal information was accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person, apply section 22 notification duties (including statutory exceptions). Notify as soon as reasonably possible; do not wait for a completed forensic report, Neo's discretionary sign-off or a made-up 72-hour rule. Record any statutory delay reason and seek urgent advice where needed. Operators notify the responsible party immediately. Initial reports can be updated as facts emerge.

Use the incident/hold row for necessary evidence, with restricted access and a documented 90-day hold review; do not use a breach to retain every candidate file indefinitely. Record releases of holds and resume the applicable review process. Test this procedure with a fictional incident before real intake. No incident, reporting action or staffed response capability is claimed.

Primary guidance: [Regulator fact sheet, 19 August 2025](https://inforegulator.org.za/2025/08/19/fact-sheet-handling-of-security-compromises/); [mandatory portal announcement, 7 April 2025](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/MEDIA-STATEMENT-INVITATION-TO-REPORT-SECURITY-COMPROMISES-THROUGH-THE-eSERVICES-PORTAL-.pdf); [POPIA](https://www.justice.gov.za/legislation/acts/2013-004.pdf), sections 21–22, 55.

## Requests and deletion process

1. Receive access, correction, objection, restriction or deletion requests at the approved contact. Do not request passwords, OTPs or unsolicited identity-document copies by email. Use a proportionate identity-check process.
2. Log the request date, affected records, responsible operator and applicable response deadline; acknowledge with a target of five business days. This is a proposed service target, not a substituted statutory deadline.
3. Review active recruitment, statutory register requirements, contracts and any documented hold. Tell the requester which categories can be removed, which must remain and why; offer restriction where applicable.
4. Prepare a dry-run inventory. The current foreign keys restrict deletion: applications and history must be handled coherently. Do not simply delete the user or strip actor links to make a database command succeed.
5. Obtain operator approval for the reviewed plan; securely delete or irreversibly de-identify eligible records, including appropriate identity-provider records and provider copies. Pseudonymised IDs that remain linkable are still personal information.
6. Verify the result, record minimal completion evidence and communicate the outcome. Keep backup-deletion markers outside the restore path and replay them before restored data is made available.

Withdrawing an application is a lifecycle action, not an account-deletion request. Removing a declared skill does not erase application history. No erasure guarantee should be published before the end-to-end process is working.
